## The one thing

Market commentary, mostly the Weekly, takes the most posting effort and returns the least. On LinkedIn it is 45% of posts but 26% of engagement, so it under-delivers. On X it is 19% of posts but 6% of engagement, which is worse. The worst LinkedIn posts in the period were both Weekly link posts, at about a seventh of a typical post. The Weekly is not failing as content. It is failing as a link with bullet points.

## Do more of

**Media appearances.** On LinkedIn they were 16% of posts and 27% of engagement, so they outperform. On X they averaged nearly 4× a typical post, and video on X ran at about 3.2× (four posts). The Bloomberg TV clip with Joseph Chalom reached almost 9×.

**Labs product posts on X.** They were 21% of posts and 53% of engagement, so they outperform. On Telegram they were 42% of posts and 56% of engagement, which also outperforms.

**Posts naming a partner.** These averaged about 2× typical on LinkedIn and X, against roughly 0.9× without a partner. On Telegram the figures were 1.4× against 0.8×.

## Do less of (or do differently)

**Chart posts.** On LinkedIn they were 14% of posts and 7% of engagement. On X they were 11% and 4%. Both under-deliver. Fold the chart into a partner-tagged or Labs post rather than posting it alone.

**Speaking events on X.** They were 16% of posts and 6% of engagement, so they under-deliver. On LinkedIn the same theme pulls its weight. Lead these on LinkedIn, and on X post them from the speaker's own profile with the organiser tagged.

**Automated Telegram posts.** "Pinned a photo" items scored zero. Overall, "other" was 6% of posts and 1% of engagement. Switch them off.

## Where the same content travels best

Research reports travel well on LinkedIn and X. The DeFi risk framework scored about 3× typical on both, but only half a typical post on Telegram. Timely pieces suit X: the Binance hack note ran at about 2.4× there and 0.6× on LinkedIn. Weeklies sit below typical everywhere, at roughly a third to a half on Telegram.

The implication is to lead with X for anything time-sensitive. On LinkedIn, research needs a native written summary rather than a link. On Telegram, long-form content needs a single takeaway and an image.

## Who moves the needle

Frax and USDai averaged about 6× typical, but on only two mentions each. Joseph Chalom (Sharplink) and Peter Smith averaged about 4.4×. Zodia Custody averaged about 3.9×, and the Zodia partnership announcement was the top LinkedIn post at about 10×. BCB Group and Tim Renew averaged about 3.5×.

Two tactics follow. Prioritise guests with their own audiences, and send partners a ready-to-repost draft on the day.

## Three changes for next week

1. **LinkedIn:** publish the Weekly as a native text post under a named author, with the partner tagged, instead of a link. This targets the 45% of posts that earn 26% of engagement.
2. **X:** drop the standalone chart of the week (11% of posts, 4% of engagement). Post one Labs vault update with Frax or USDai tagged instead.
3. **Telegram:** turn off automated pin posts, and move Labs updates to Friday, when posts average about 1.8× typical (11 posts).

## Something I did not expect

On X, speaking-event posts are not dull. They earn about 11 likes per 1,000 views, better than media appearances at 7. They simply reach few people, with a median of 398 views against 1,970 for media appearances. The problem is distribution, not content.

## Caveats

Several figures are directional because they rest on fewer than three posts. These include the LinkedIn partner announcement (one post), LinkedIn video (two), and the Frax and USDai figures (two mentions each).

LinkedIn provides no impressions, so the figures there show reactions only. The Telegram score is views alone. The latest weeks contain only one to three posts, and day-of-week patterns may simply reflect what was posted on those days.

These are associations, not proof of cause. No system-generated experiments have been scored yet.
