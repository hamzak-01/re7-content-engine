## The one thing

The Weekly, filed as market commentary, takes the largest share of posting effort and returns the least. On LinkedIn it is 45% of posts but 26% of engagement (return on effort 0.58). On X it is 19% of posts and 6% of engagement (0.30). Posts that name a partner score roughly double those that do not: 1.98 against 0.83 on LinkedIn and 1.99 against 0.97 on X. The Weekly should lead with a named partner or person rather than a generic headline.

## Do more of

**Labs product posts on X.** They make up 21% of posts and 55% of engagement (2.61), with the strongest resonance of any theme at 15.6 likes per 1,000 views. On Telegram the same theme earns 1.35.

**Media appearances.** They score 1.70 on LinkedIn and 1.40 on X, with an X mean index of 3.85.

**Video.** It averages 2.86 on LinkedIn and 3.18 on X, although there are only four posts on each.

## Do less of (or do differently)

**Chart data.** It returns 0.48 on LinkedIn and 0.34 on X. Fold the chart into the Weekly rather than posting it on its own.

**Speaking events on X.** They return 0.38 across 10 posts, against 1.18 on LinkedIn. Keep them on LinkedIn and post them from the speaker's own profile.

**Telegram "other" posts.** These return 0.20, and the "pinned a photo" notices score 0.0. Stop them.

## Where the same content travels best

Research travels well and Weeklies do not. The DeFi-risk framework scored 2.97 on LinkedIn and 3.09 on X but only 0.59 on Telegram. Weeklies sat at or below 1.0 on most channels. The Binance piece was the exception on X at 2.36, which suggests a timely news hook is what lifts a Weekly there.

Telegram is weakest for every shared piece (0.11 to 0.59), and its weekly trend has fallen from 0.52 to 0.11. Telegram needs its own product-led version rather than a repost.
